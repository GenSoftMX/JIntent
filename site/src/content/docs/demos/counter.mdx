The counter is the smallest complete JIntent app. A tap calls a controller method, the controller runs an intent, the intent validates through a use case, persists through a repository and updates the state. Rejections do not touch the state: they travel as effects.

<DemoFrame route="/counter" title="Counter demo" height={520} />

## Try it

1. Press **Increment** a few times (a 200 ms throttle ignores presses that come faster). Each press runs an `IncrementIntent` that updates `count` in the state.
2. Reaching 10 emits a `ShowSuccessEffect`: a one-time "Milestone reached" snackbar that is not part of the state.
3. Keep pressing **Increment** at 10. The count stays at 10, and on every second call (a stateful validator, on purpose) the use case rejects the value. The rejection is a `ShowRejectOperation` **effect** shown as a red snackbar, not a change of state.

## What to look at in the code

- The intents under [`presentation/counter/intents`](https://github.com/GenSoftMX/JIntent/tree/main/example/lib/src/presentation/counter/intents) fold an `Either` from the use case into an update or an effect.
- [`counter_effect_handler.dart`](https://github.com/GenSoftMX/JIntent/blob/main/example/lib/src/presentation/counter/presentation/counter_effect_handler.dart) maps every effect type to its UI.
- This demo reads state with Riverpod; the other demos use `JControllerBuilder`. Both work because a `JController` is a `StateNotifier`.
